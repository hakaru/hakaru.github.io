Before you start

Tonrad is a standalone tonewheel organ for iPhone and iPad running iOS/iPadOS 18 or later. Turn your device sideways: the app works in landscape only. Its three screens are HOME, DRAWBARS and SET. The app interface is available in Japanese and English; this manual is available in 11 languages.

Check your version. As of November 15, 2026, the App Store version is 0.1.0. This manual also describes the 0.2.0 update. Features marked 0.2.0 are not available in 0.1.0. The screenshots show 0.1.0.

There are no presets, recording, accounts, ads or in-app purchases. Tonrad does not run as an Audio Unit plug-in. PRESET and TUNE are not screens in the App Store edition.

Play your first notes

  1. Open Tonrad with your device in landscape. Start with a comfortable device volume.
  2. On HOME, play the top keyboard, labelled UPPER. The initial drawbar settings already produce a sound; no external keyboard is needed.
  3. Move the EXP slider on the right upward if the sound is too quiet. Check SET → AUDIO → OUTPUT and the displayed output device if you still hear nothing.
  4. Tap DRAWBARS. To try a simple tone, set UPPER's 8′ drawbar to 8, the other eight UPPER drawbars to 0, and PERC to OFF. Return to HOME and play.
  5. Add the UPPER 16′ and 5⅓′ drawbars, then try ROTARY → SLOW / FAST.

LOWER and PEDAL have their own drawbars. Raising a UPPER drawbar does not give the other keyboards a sound.

HOME: keyboards and expression

The three rows are UPPER, LOWER and PEDAL. Press a key to play; release it to stop. Sliding a finger across the keys changes the note. Multiple fingers can play at once.

On a smaller screen, a narrow bar above each manual keyboard shows its visible range. Tap or drag that bar horizontally to reach lower or higher notes. It moves the visible keys, not their tuning. A wide screen can show the full manual, so the range bars disappear. The pedalboard remains a separate row.

EXP is the expression control for the whole organ. Drag up for more sound and down for less; a tap alone does not jump to that level. Double-tap EXP to restore maximum. It starts at maximum each time you launch the app and is not saved. Use it for musical swells; use SET → OUTPUT for the overall output level.

DRAWBARS: build a sound

Drag a drawbar downward to increase its level, or upward to reduce it. Each has positions 0–8: 0 is silent and 8 is maximum. UPPER and LOWER each have nine drawbars; PEDAL has two.

BankDrawbars, left to right
UPPER / LOWER16′ · 5⅓′ · 8′ · 4′ · 2⅔′ · 2′ · 1⅗′ · 1⅓′ · 1′
PEDAL16′ · 8′

The 8′ drawbar gives the played note's pitch; 16′ adds an octave below and 4′ an octave above. The shorter and fractional footages add higher harmonics. Combine their levels to shape the tone.

Try 8 8 8 0 0 0 0 0 0 on UPPER as a starting registration. These numbers describe positions from left to right; they are an example to set by hand, not a preset you can select. Reduce OUTPUT if a denser registration becomes too loud.

Percussion: an accent on UPPER

Percussion adds a decaying accent to the upper manual. Its controls are above the drawbars:

  • PERC — ON / OFF: enable or disable the accent.
  • VOLUME — SOFT / NORMAL: choose its level.
  • DECAY — FAST / SLOW: choose how quickly the accent fades.
  • HARMONIC — 2ND / 3RD: choose the second or third harmonic.

Percussion retriggers after you release all UPPER keys and play again. Legato playing with a key held does not retrigger it on every note. When PERC is ON, the UPPER 1′ drawbar is dimmed and does not sound; its position is retained for when you turn percussion off.

Vibrato and chorus

VIB UPPER and VIB LOWER enable the effect for UPPER and for LOWER/PEDAL respectively. Choose one shared mode from V1, V2, V3, C1, C2, C3.

V modes add vibrato; C modes mix the original sound with the vibrato signal for chorus. The three numbers select different depths. Selecting a mode does not turn an OFF manual switch on. VIB LOWER also affects PEDAL. Percussion is not passed through this vibrato effect.

Rotary speaker and spring reverb

On HOME, tap the large ROTARY control to alternate between SLOW and FAST. The change accelerates or slows down rather than jumping instantly.

  • 0.1.0: the separate SPEAKER ON / OFF button bypasses the whole rotary speaker when OFF.
  • 0.2.0: ROTOR ON / OFF stops or starts rotation while keeping the speaker sound. To bypass the entire speaker, enable SET → AUDIO → SPEAKER OFF. Turn that switch off to hear the speaker again.

In DRAWBARS, the REV drawbar to the right of PEDAL controls spring reverb. Its range is 0–8; 0 turns it off. Start with 2, then adjust to taste. Reverb is separate from the rotary speaker.

SET: output, PANIC and audio information

AUDIO shows the current output device and the OUTPUT slider. Adjust this for the overall level. Double-tap the OUTPUT label/value area to restore its default. The slider itself does not respond to that reset gesture.

PANIC stops all sounding notes. Use it if a note hangs after disconnecting a keyboard or changing audio connections.

NUMBERS shows audio information such as sample rate, buffer size and processing counters. These are readouts, not audio configuration controls. RESET resets the host's diagnostic measurements, not your sound or saved settings. ENGINE DROPS and REPAIRS remain until the audio engine restarts.

The privacy-policy link opens the website in your browser. In 0.2.0, SET also contains the MIDI settings described below. You may need to scroll to see all of them.

USB MIDI keyboards — 0.2.0

  1. Connect a USB MIDI keyboard to the iPhone or iPad with a suitable data cable or adapter. A device that needs more power may require a powered hub.
  2. Open SET → MIDI and check the connected-device list.
  3. By default, send on CH 1 for UPPER, CH 2 for LOWER or CH 3 for PEDAL.
  4. Set the receiving channel for each part in SET if needed. Channels stay distinct: choosing a channel used by another part exchanges their assignments.
  5. Use OCT for −3 to +3 octaves of shift within the zones described below. It applies to notes routed from the UPPER input channel, not to the onscreen keyboards or direct LOWER/PEDAL-channel notes.

Tonrad receives the MIDI inputs available to iOS. There is no Bluetooth MIDI pairing screen inside the app. In 0.2.0, audio and MIDI can continue while you view another app. Version 0.1.0 accepts no MIDI input and stops its audio when moved to the background.

One keyboard, three zones — 0.2.0

For one keyboard, send on the channel assigned to UPPER. The strip along the top divides that input into BASS / LOWER / UPPER. With both boundaries at the far left, the full range goes to UPPER.

  1. Drag from the strip's left edge to the right to create a LOWER/UPPER boundary.
  2. Drag the boundary still at the left edge rightward to create a BASS zone. The order is BASS, LOWER, UPPER.
  3. Drag a zone vertically to adjust its octave, or use its OCT control in SET. These octave shifts apply to notes from the UPPER input channel.

Fold notes into range in SET brings MIDI notes outside a part's playable range back by octaves, keeping their note name. It also works for direct LOWER/PEDAL-channel input. Changes apply to subsequent notes.

Add a pedal tap to left-hand bass sends the BASS zone to LOWER and adds a short pedal tone with the same note name. It also applies to LOWER notes below the BASS boundary. Move that boundary away from the far left to use it. Shape the added tone with the PEDAL drawbars and adjust Tap length in SET. Changes apply to subsequent notes.

Pedals, knobs and MIDI LEARN — 0.2.0

The default controls are CC11 for expression, CC1 for rotary speed (0–63 SLOW, 64–127 FAST) and CC64 to toggle SLOW/FAST. These default controls respond on the channels assigned to the three parts. CC64 is a speed switch here, not a piano sustain pedal: pressing toggles the speed and releasing does not change it back.

  1. Open SET → MIDI LEARN, or hold a drawbar, EXP, REV, ROTARY or ROTOR still for about 0.6 seconds to open its assignment. For percussion and vibrato, use the SET list.
  2. If you opened the list from SET, select the row you want to control. A long press has already selected it and started learning; do not tap that row again, as this cancels learning. Move a knob, slider or button on your MIDI device.
  3. Check that the row shows CH … · CC …, then close the list and try the control.

Drawbars, EXP, REV, percussion, vibrato and rotary controls can be assigned. Use the row's × button to remove an assignment; Clear All removes every learned assignment. The assignments are saved locally. Learning the same channel/CC for a different target moves the assignment to that target.

CC1, CC64 and CC120–127 cannot be learned. CC11 can be assigned only to EXP. SLOW/FAST and ROTOR toggle when a value crosses from 63 or below to 64 or above; releasing does not toggle them back. Other learned switches use 64–127 for ON and 0–63 for OFF.

What is saved

Tonrad restores drawbar positions, percussion, vibrato, rotary-speaker settings, reverb, visible keyboard ranges and OUTPUT on your next launch. In 0.2.0, MIDI channels, zones, octave shifts, range-foldback/pedal-tap settings and MIDI LEARN assignments are saved too.

EXP is not saved and starts at maximum. There are no named presets or setting-export/cloud-sync features. Settings stay on your device; deleting the app deletes them.

How the tonewheel engine works

A tonewheel organ generates electrical tones with rotating toothed metal wheels and electromagnetic pickups. Pressing a key connects selected tones to the output; it does not start the wheels. Drawbars set the balance of these components. The nine contacts under each manual key do not necessarily close together, and their brief opening and closing contributes to key click.

Background reading: Moro, McPherson & Sandler (2017) · Werner & Abel (2016).

Tonrad models this signal path in software. One bank of 91 tonewheels is shared by the upper manual, lower manual and pedals. It is not a separate bank of 91 oscillators for every played note. Each manual has 9 contacts per key; the pedals use 8 contacts and 4 busbars mixed into 2 drawbars. Near the limits of the generator, manual wiring reuses tones in another octave (foldback).

Implementation choices in Tonrad

Continuous rotation and shared shafts
The engine advances 48 shaft phases while audio processing runs. Wheels mounted on the same modeled shaft share its motion and slow pitch fluctuations. Note-on does not reset their phase, so successive attacks can meet different points of the waveform.
Frequency from gearing
Frequency is calculated from motor speed, gear ratio and tooth count, rather than forcing every wheel to an exact equal-tempered frequency. For example, 20 revolutions/s × 88/64 × 16 teeth gives 440 Hz.
A waveform for a whole revolution
Tonrad builds each wheel’s periodic waveform from harmonics, level differences, amplitude wobble and small tooth-phase irregularities. The lowest 12 wheels use a richer odd-harmonic shape. These are synthesized waveforms, not recordings of complete played notes.
Controlled irregularity and signal processing
Fixed seeds preserve each wheel’s and key’s character. The divisions have separate random streams for contact timing, so playing one division does not shift another division’s bounce sequence. Wheel tables are band-limited; contact switching receives separate corrections. Leakage and load-dependent level changes also affect the combined organ tone.

This is a designed digital model, not a measurement of every component of one particular vintage organ. In particular, the contact-time defaults below are Tonrad’s tuning choices, not universal specifications for mechanical organs.

Attack timing: contacts, velocity and key click

Tonrad gives each contact its own switching time. A low drawbar can therefore begin later than a high one, changing the spectrum during the attack. This is different from applying one shared volume envelope to the completed note. Velocity controls the contact-time spread; it does not directly scale the sustained organ level like a piano velocity curve.

For a note-on velocity V from 1 to 127, the first-to-last contact spread S is calculated in milliseconds as follows. Velocity 0 means note-off.

v = (V − 1) / 126
S = slow × (fast / slow) ^ (v ^ curve)

slow = spread_slow_ms, fast = spread_fast_ms, curve = spread_curve. At V = 1, S equals slow; at V = 127, it equals fast. With slow greater than fast, a larger curve keeps intermediate velocities closer to the slower attack. The default values are 91.9 ms, 4.55 ms and 1.52. S measures the first-to-last initial closure, not the time until all contacts settle: the last contact can still bounce after S.

Which contact closes first?

Upper manual
The base order is contact 9 → 1: 1′ → 1⅓′ → 1⅗′ → 2′ → 2⅔′ → 4′ → 8′ → 5⅓′ → 16′. With key_jitter = 0, contact c closes at t = (9 − c) / 8 × S. Thus 8′ begins at 6/8 of S and 16′ at S; a 16′-only registration waits the full spread.
Lower manual
Contact 6 (2′) is first and contact 1 (16′) last. The base position is |c − 6| / 5. Contacts 5/7, 4/8 and 3/9 share base positions; per-key variation can put either contact of a pair first. The intermediate spacing is a modeling choice.
Pedals
The 8 contacts use the base position (8 − c) / 7: contact 8 closes first and contact 1, carrying the fundamental, closes last. This ordering is a design assumption, not a documented timing measurement of a mechanical pedalboard.

The default key_jitter = 0.5 shifts intermediate contact positions by a fixed amount for each key. The first and last contacts stay fixed. Upper and pedal contact order is preserved. This per-key variation is different from the bounce pattern, which can change on each strike.

Bounce, release and the sound of the attack

When a contact connects a running waveform at a nonzero value, the signal changes abruptly. Brief reopening and reclosing adds more transitions. Tonrad generates click from these switches instead of adding a separate recorded click. It corrects jumps in both value and slope to reduce digital aliasing while retaining the transient.

An early bounce burst uses up to 4 bounce pairs by default, with opening and reclosing gaps drawn between 20 and 400 µs and a 2 ms burst limit. A separate late-bounce process has probability 0.15 and may reopen a contact 3–8 ms after its first closure. These distributions are model choices.

Note-off cancels future closures and opens contacts that are already closed in the reverse modeled order, over 12 ms by default. A very short note may finish before its later, lower components ever connect. A quick retrigger also respects contacts that have not yet opened. This is not a new, identical attack waveform on every key press.

Internal contact events retain fractional-sample timing. The switching correction delays the direct engine signal by 8 samples (about 0.167 ms at 48 kHz). This is separate from contact spread, audio buffering and the speaker path. The current iOS MIDI path consumes events at render boundaries; it does not preserve incoming MIDI timestamps as sample offsets.

What you can try in the app

  1. To hear the organ’s attack more directly, turn PERC and both VIB switches off and set REV to 0. Bypass the speaker with HOME → SPEAKER OFF in 0.1.0, or SET → AUDIO → SPEAKER OFF enabled in 0.2.0. Keep EXP and OUTPUT at a consistent, comfortable level.
  2. On UPPER, compare 8′ alone, 16′ alone and a mixture with higher drawbars. Compare a held note with a short tap. The sounding components connect at different times; the drawbars change which part of that process you hear. PERC adds a separate decaying accent and is not the contact-spread control.
  3. In 0.2.0, use an external MIDI keyboard that sends different velocities. Compare soft and strong strikes with the same registration. In 0.1.0, or on the touchscreen in either version, velocity stays at 100, so varying touch pressure cannot perform this comparison.

Reference for a development build with TUNE

Only a DEBUG build launched in the Pro development mode exposes TUNE → Contacts. Start with the defaults and change one parameter at a time while repeating the same note and velocity. Compare weak-strike spread first, then strong-strike spread and curve. To isolate timing from bounce, set bounce_max = 0 and late_bounce_prob = 0; setting only bounce_burst_ms = 0 leaves the late process enabled. There is no instruction here to unlock or purchase this screen in the public app.

Troubleshooting

No sound
Check device volume, the output device shown in SET, EXP and OUTPUT. Raise at least one drawbar in the part you are playing. With PERC on, UPPER's 1′ drawbar alone will not produce a sustained tone. If audio was interrupted, return to Tonrad and try again.
A note will not stop
Tap SET → PANIC. If using MIDI, check the keyboard connection before playing again.
MIDI device is missing or silent
First check that you have 0.2.0. Look for the device in SET, check its power/data connection and match the transmitting and receiving channels. Check the zone boundaries, OCT and drawbars for the destination part.
High or low MIDI notes are missing
Review OCT and the split boundaries. In 0.2.0, try range foldback for notes outside the organ's playable range.
Rotary motion has disappeared
In 0.1.0, turn SPEAKER ON. In 0.2.0, turn SPEAKER OFF off in SET, then turn ROTOR ON on HOME. SLOW produces a subtler movement than FAST.
The screen will not turn portrait
Landscape-only operation is intentional.
I cannot find PRESET, TUNE or recording
These are not available in the App Store edition.

For help, include your Tonrad version, device, iOS/iPadOS version, the screen/control involved and the steps that led to the problem. For MIDI issues, also include your keyboard model and connection method.
