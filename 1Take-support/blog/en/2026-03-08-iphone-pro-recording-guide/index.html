The Complete Guide to Professional iPhone Recording

March 8, 2026

Introduction

Your iPhone is more capable than you think. With the right techniques and tools, you can capture studio-quality recordings wherever you are. This guide covers everything from microphone placement to signal processing.

1. Understanding Your iPhone's Microphone

Modern iPhones have multiple microphones positioned strategically around the device. The main bottom microphone is designed for voice calls, while the top microphone helps with noise cancellation and stereo recording.

Key Limitations to Know

  • AGC (Automatic Gain Control): iPhone's built-in system constantly adjusts input volume, which ruins dynamic performances.
  • Frequency response: Apple applies processing to make vocals sound "clean" — but this can strip character from instruments.
  • Proximity effect: Close-mic recording introduces low-frequency buildup. Use EQ to compensate.

2. Microphone Placement Techniques

For Vocals

  • Hold the iPhone 20-30cm from your mouth, slightly off-axis to reduce plosives
  • Avoid pointing the mic directly at your mouth — angle it slightly to the side
  • Use a windscreen or pop filter if recording in treated spaces

For Acoustic Guitar

  • Point the mic toward the 12th fret, not the soundhole — the soundhole creates boomy, muddy low-end
  • Distance of 30-50cm gives a natural room sound
  • Closer = more detail, further = more air and room

For Piano

  • Open the lid slightly and position iPhone above the strings
  • Experiment with left vs right side for bass-heavy vs treble-heavy sound

For Drums and Loud Sources

  • Distance is your friend — 2-3 meters captures the natural room sound
  • Use compression (1176-style preset in 1Take) to control transient peaks

3. Room Treatment and Environment

Your recording space matters more than your microphone. Hard surfaces create reflections that muddy recordings.

Quick Acoustic Treatment

  • Closets: Hanging clothes are natural absorbers — great for vocals
  • Corners: Avoid them — bass frequencies build up there
  • Soft furniture: Couches, carpets, and curtains absorb high frequencies
  • Outside or outdoors: No reflections, but wind noise is a challenge

4. Signal Processing Chain

With 1Take, your iPhone processes audio through a professional chain before recording:

  1. Input Trim: Set proper gain before anything else. Target -18dBFS for peaks.
  2. Noise Gate: Eliminates hiss and background noise between phrases.
  3. 4-Band EQ: Cut problematic frequencies, boost what enhances the instrument.
  4. 2-Stage Compression: Comp A catches transients, Comp B adds density.
  5. Maximizer: Prevents digital clipping — your last line of defense.

5. Choosing the Right Preset

1Take's presets are modeled after legendary gear for specific use cases:

  • Studio (LA-2A style): Vocals, acoustic guitar, piano. Smooth optical compression with warm character.
  • Studio+ (1176 style): Electric guitar, drums, bass. Aggressive FET compression with punch.
  • Live (VCA style): Rehearsals, live capture, loud environments. Fast, transparent control.

6. Monitoring and Levels

Watch the VU meter while recording. The needle should average around the 0VU mark with peaks reaching +3 to +6VU. If the needle is consistently pegged to the right, reduce Input Trim.

Use the GR (Gain Reduction) meter to see how hard the compressor is working. 3-6dB of gain reduction is musical. More than 10dB usually means your input is too hot.

7. Exporting and DAW Integration

1Take Pro records in 24-bit BWF (Broadcast Wave Format). This professional format embeds timestamps directly in the file, making sync work in DAWs like Logic Pro or DaVinci Resolve effortless.

For casual sharing, export to MP3 or AAC directly from the Recordings Library.
